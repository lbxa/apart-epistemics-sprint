\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}
\textbf{Benchmark work as an evidential anchor.}
FinQA~\cite{chen2021} and TAT-QA~\cite{zhu2021} ground financial calculations
in reports. ContractNLI~\cite{koreeda2021} supplies contract inference labels;
SciFact~\cite{wadden2020} supplies scientific claims and annotated evidence;
MathDial~\cite{macina2023} supplies mathematical tutoring dialogues. We reuse
their material and references, adding disputes. Our adapted scalar and label
endpoints are narrower than their full evaluations.

\textbf{Confidence and answer revision.}
Self-assessment~\cite{kadavath2022} and verbal calibration~\cite{tian2023}
motivate prospective probabilities, without identifying internal belief.
Sycophancy~\cite{sharma2024} motivates evidence-free pressure controls;
self-correction~\cite{huang2024} motivates comparing challenge with neutral
review. Our audit directs attention within supplied evidence, adding no
independent observations.

\textbf{Evaluating the evaluator.}
LLM-as-a-judge research documents agreement and judge biases~\cite{zheng2023}.
We separate support judgments from reference grades, hide forecasts and
predeclared a cross-family replay. Three calls can share an error. Veracity
predates this sprint; the present adaptation and study are new.

LiveBench refreshes questions from recent sources to reduce
contamination~\cite{white2024}. Our objection audit concerns a different risk:
an evaluator can encode an answer in material created during the test itself.
For completed-action claims, $\tau$-bench checks final database state against
an annotated goal~\cite{yao2024}; our current text tasks do not provide that
execution-grounded endpoint.
