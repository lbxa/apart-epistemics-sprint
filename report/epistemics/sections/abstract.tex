Stated confidence, a defended answer and a jury verdict measure different
aspects of model epistemics. We separate them by adding controlled disputes
to existing benchmark tasks. Five model families answer 120 source-based
cases across finance, legal reasoning, scientific evidence and educational
arithmetic before Veracity is disclosed. Frozen answers receive four challenge
conditions with five defence seeds each; correctness and jury-acceptance
forecasts have separate targets. Of 12,000 assigned branches, 11,620 execute,
yielding 11,483 valid defences and 11,469 settled verdicts. Under a post hoc
source-audit sensitivity, answers lose reference agreement in 20/2,543 pressure
branches, while audit repairs 17/334 initial mismatches. Mean initial confidence
exceeds reference accuracy for four of five models in legal reasoning and
all five in science. A selected arithmetic diagnostic provides a directly
checkable failure: adding the author's false defence reduces correct rejection
from 15/15 to 3/15 for DeepSeek and from 14/15 to 0/15 for GPT-OSS; appending
verified arithmetic restores 15/15 for both. These standalone calls supplement
an executed unanimous verdict upholding the false claim, without estimating a general effect.
Auditing eight numerical cases changes 30 initial grades, and an offline
inverse recovers 287 stored references from scripted objections. We retain
literal grades and disclose these evaluator defects. Source-group intervals
and five-seed variation distinguish repeated generations from distinct sources.
The results separate stability, source agreement and acceptance without
identifying internal beliefs, deceptive intent or representative industry performance.
